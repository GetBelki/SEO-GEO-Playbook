# Comparison pages and guides

For anyone planning or writing "X vs Y" pages, "best X for Y" pages, buying guides or product documentation for a shop.

## Rules
- **Publish neutral comparison pages for the top head-to-heads in each category.** *Measured, B2B; transfer to ecommerce is inference* ([§1](#1-comparisons-are-what-assistants-cite)).
- **Lead with the verdict, then a comparison table, then "best for" use cases, then links on to category and product pages**; on editorial guides, make the headline's first words specific and put a summary sentence right after the H1. *Measured, engagement only*; headlines *Practitioner, publisher context* ([§2](#2-verdict-table-best-for-onward-links), [§9](#9-headlines-and-summaries-for-editorial-guides)).
- **Publish buying guides and manuals as crawlable HTML pages, not only PDFs.** *Inference from a SERP sighting*; PDF deindexing *Practitioner observation* ([§3](#3-guides-and-documentation)).
- **Prioritise decision-stage content (price, cost, comparisons) over generic how-tos.** *Measured, client blogs* ([§3](#3-guides-and-documentation)).
- **Cover one topic per page, in depth** — neither thin pages nor catch-all guides — and set no word-count targets. *Measured, partly headline only; correlation* ([§4](#4-one-topic-in-depth-dated-honestly)).
- **Keep "as of" dates, `dateModified` and sitemap `lastmod` true and visible, and refresh data-driven and repeatedly cited comparison pages with real changes to the main content — never a date bump alone, never a fake date.** *Measured (assistants cite fresher pages), vendor and agency data, correlation; Patent; From code; no evidence-based cadence* ([§4](#4-one-topic-in-depth-dated-honestly)).
- **Consolidate fragmented FAQ and keyword-variant pages into one guide with anchors and a table of contents; keep comparisons only with transparent criteria and real trade-offs, and never publish "according to X" rankings that feature your own products.** *Practitioner; search extracts; Official statements, relayed; Measured, small samples* ([§5](#5-consolidate-and-earn-links)).
- **Build FAQs from real support questions as visible text, without counting on the FAQ rich result.** *Practitioner*; drop *relayed* ([§6](#6-faq-content-after-the-rich-result)).
- **Write each section as a self-contained passage: descriptive heading, the entity named, the answering sentence first.** *Network capture, 1 prompt; small reranker test* ([§7](#7-passages-that-answer-on-their-own)).
- **Put the answer, specs and differentiators in the first ~500 words, and protect the one sentence that answers the query.** *Measured, private data, directional; one-page ablation* ([§7](#7-passages-that-answer-on-their-own)).
- **Publish original, sourced statistics and quotes for your category**, with source and year next to each number, and check each guide still says something only you could say once the logo is removed. *Practitioner, one-site case; academic, relayed; essay* ([§8](#8-original-statistics-pages), [§10](#10-the-strip-the-logo-test)).

## Checklist
- [ ] The first sentence after the H1 is the verdict, with the entity names spelled out.
- [ ] A comparison table with the criteria that decide the purchase, and real trade-offs.
- [ ] "Best for" use cases for each option.
- [ ] Links on to the category and product pages for each option.
- [ ] The page is HTML (a PDF may sit alongside), linked internally, not sitemap-only.
- [ ] One topic; each section self-contained under a descriptive heading.
- [ ] Visible "as of" or updated date matches `dateModified` and the last real edit; data-driven pages have a refresh owner and schedule.
- [ ] No second page serves the same intent.
- [ ] FAQ answers come from support questions and are visible text.
- [ ] Each section's first sentence answers one sub-question and names the product or entity (no "it" or "this").
- [ ] Verdict, key specs and differentiators sit in the first ~500 words; promotional lines off the topic are cut.
- [ ] Long guides have a table of contents and anchor links per section; overlapping pages tested by SERP overlap before merging or splitting.
- [ ] No ranking page "according to" a testing organisation lists your own products; your own comparison is dated, states its method and says it is yours.
- [ ] Data pages give source and year next to every number.
- [ ] Traffic-driving PDF guides have an HTML version; PDF index status checked.
- [ ] `dateModified` and sitemap `lastmod` change only with a real edit; comparison pages that keep getting cited are on a refresh list.
- [ ] Editorial guides: the headline's first words name the subject; a summary sentence follows the H1; subheads phrased as questions where they are questions.
- [ ] With the logo removed, the guide still contains something only you could publish (own data, tests, photos, opinion).

## 1. Comparisons are what assistants cite
- For ~1,000 bottom-funnel prompts, comparison pages were the most-cited page type, ahead of listicles; product pages and homepages were cited at single-digit rates.[^siege-bofu]
- Across 116 B2B sites, the number of "X vs Y" pages correlated with AI sessions at Spearman 0.65, about double the next content type; content explained only ~28 % of the variance between sites.[^siege-versus]
- Discovery and evaluation pages take about 57.0 % of citations but 8.9 % of AI traffic.[^aleyda-traffic] Judge these pages by citations and onward clicks, not their own sessions. Tracking: [AI visibility tracking](../measure/ai-visibility-tracking.md).

## 2. Verdict, table, best for, onward links
- "Best X" pages with sessions over 150 s consistently used comparison tables, and most visits saw one page only.[^siege-best-x] Give onward paths.
- ChatGPT reads ~200 characters from the H1; Gemini takes 80 % of cited passages from the first half of the page.[^chatgpt-retrieval][^gemini-convo] Put the verdict there ([AI assistants](../how-it-works/ai-assistants.md)).

## 3. Guides and documentation
- AI Mode shows rich carousels for "expert buying guide" and "product documentation" (Jul 2026).[^brodie-notes]
- **PDFs fell out of the index.** From about 25 August 2026 Google deindexed PDFs at scale ("Crawled – currently not indexed"); some were later reindexed. The URL Inspection API allows 2,000 URLs per property per day, counted separately per property, so directory properties multiply the quota for checking them. *Practitioner observation, client data.*[^gg-pdf] **Do:** convert traffic-driving PDF guides and manuals to HTML pages; keep the PDF as a download ([Search Console](../measure/search-console.md)).
- Each category has one main buyer uncertainty, and AI answers cite guides and support pages to resolve it.[^aleyda-ecom] One guide per uncertainty, linked from its [category page](category-pages.md).
- Blog traffic share moved toward pricing/cost (+12.1 points) and comparison (+2.3) and away from guides (−6.9) and how-tos (−8.3), Aug 2023 – Aug 2025.[^siege-blog-shift]
- Pages that rose in a core update answered the follow-up questions and had troubleshooting sections and FAQs (5 cases).[^mh-june2025] Find missing sub-questions with a [fan-out check](../measure/diagnostic-tests.md#13-fan-out-coverage).

## 4. One topic in depth, dated honestly
- Shorter, focused pages got cited more in ChatGPT across 815,000 query-page pairs. *Headline only.*[^gm-shorter]

> **Conflict:** another analysis found pages over 20,000 characters cited far more often than short ones — [details](../reference/conflicts.md#short-or-long-pages)

- **No length targets.** Across ~900M pages, referring domains rose with length up to ~1,000 words and then fell; organic traffic rose moderately, then turned negative above ~2,000 words. *Measured, correlation, 2020 data.* Google's John Mueller (2019): word count isn't a ranking factor.[^ah-length] Cover what the top pages share, briefly.
- Freshness is checked late in AI retrieval, "a reflection-stage gate". *Practitioner estimate, no method.*[^ipr-agentic-rag]
- **Assistants cite fresher content.** Across ~16.975M cited URLs, AI-assistant citations averaged 1,064 days since publication vs 1,432 for organic results; ChatGPT's citations were 458 days newer than organic ones, while AI Overview citations were 16 days older. *Measured, vendor data.*[^ah-freshness]
- **What counts as a change.** A Google patent on historical data counts changes to boilerplate, navigation, ads, scripts, comments and date tags for little, and title and main-content changes for more; for some queries pages that stay unchanged rank better. *Patent (likely expired); the 2024 leak supports the concept with several date attributes.*[^sbs-freshness] Don't force freshness on evergreen queries.
- **AI rerankers favour recent dates.** ChatGPT's search config (Aug 2025) shows `use_freshness_scoring_profile: true` and a reranker `ret-rr-skysight-v3`. *From code.*[^mt-config] In a peer study, LLM rerankers moved their top 10 toward newer dates by +0.82 to +4.78 years from injected dates alone, with up to 95-rank jumps and 8.25–25.23 % preference reversals. *Measured, 7 LLMs.*[^mt-recency] Dates alone moved those rankings, so never fake one: it misleads readers and breaks the true-date rule above. **Do:** a visible updated date, matched by a real edit.
- **Refresh with real changes, not a new date.** Google's John Mueller warns against date-only updates.[^ah-freshness] There is no evidence-based refresh cadence; Ahrefs moved 14 of its own datasets from quarterly to monthly refreshes, with no measured result reported. *Practitioner, own case.*[^ah-refresh-case] **Do:** give each data-driven guide an owner and a refresh schedule that matches how fast its data changes.
- **Cited pages are updated, not necessarily new.** Across 4 brands (one in pet retail; 47,097 citations in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, Mar–Jun 2026; dates read from schema, sitemaps and headers), 75 % of cited pages had been updated within the past year but only 42 % were published within it (subset of 4,124 pages), and over 25 % of "fresh" pages were first published 2+ years earlier. Updated within a year, by engine: Gemini 78 %, ChatGPT 73 %, Perplexity 65 %; by type: comparison/review pages 77 %, blogs/guides 67 %. Pages cited once had a median update age of 0.16 years, pages cited every month 0.47 years. *Measured, agency data, correlational.*[^seer-recency] **Do:** keep `dateModified` and sitemap `lastmod` accurate, refresh comparison pages that keep getting cited with real changes, and never change the dates alone.

## 5. Consolidate and earn links
- Fold fragmented FAQ and keyword-variant pages that serve one intent into the canonical page; keep comparisons only with transparent criteria and real trade-offs; keep calculators only with proprietary data or a product action.[^aleyda-priorities]
- Proprietary data, tools and calculators, buyer's guides and niche checklists earn links naturally; ecommerce sites earned about 8.7 new referring domains per 10,000 organic visits. *Measured, 100 sites, tool undisclosed.*[^siege-links]
- **One guide with anchors, not micro-pages.** Google's Danny Sullivan (Jan 2026) advised against bite-sized chunks, and Bing (Dec 2025) said near-duplicates blur signals. *Official statements, relayed.* Test with SERP overlap: if two topics share results, consolidate; if not, keep them separate. Give the guide a table of contents, anchor links, the direct answer in the first two lines of each section, and tables for comparisons. *Practitioner.*[^nz-chunk] Same rule for templates: [scaling content and templates](../situations/scaling-content-and-templates.md).
- **No "according to X" rankings of your own products.** When answers' searches named a testing authority, only 37 % (58/158) cited that authority's own site; retailer and affiliate "<authority> best buy" pages often misquote it. *Measured, small samples, Gemini.*[^nz-model] **Do:** don't publish rankings attributed to an outside authority that feature your own products; if no one covers you, publish a dated, method-disclosed comparison labelled as your own. Getting covered by real reviewers: [third-party presence](../channels/third-party-presence.md).
- **Data-led assets for PR.** How to turn such data into pitches — pitch lists built from publishers AI assistants cite, regional data comparisons for local journalists: [links and digital PR](../channels/links-and-digital-pr.md).

## 6. FAQ content after the rich result
- FAQ rich results were reported dropped on 2026-05-07. *Relayed.*[^ignite-news] FAQ content still serves readers and assistants.
- Build FAQs from real support questions; the answers must be visible text.[^ai-visible]

## 7. Passages that answer on their own
- **ChatGPT scores fetched pages chunk by chunk.** In one captured comparison prompt, ChatGPT ran 4 searches and fetched 96 pages; each fetched page carried one neural score per chunk, max-normalised so the best chunk = 1.0. The ~200-character card is the search-results view; the fetched page is judged per passage. *Network capture, 1 prompt.*[^mt-chunks]
- **Direct answers win rerankers.** Open rerankers scored a direct-answer passage above 99.9 % against a descriptive one (MiniLM-L6: 0.0019 %; Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B: 0.27 %); missing query concepts hurt more than word order. *Measured, small controlled test, open models — no evidence ChatGPT uses them.*[^mt-rerank] **Do:** name the entity in each section, cover the decision criteria, and put the answering sentence first.
- **The first ~500 words carry the page.** Google grounding gave each query a fixed budget (median 1,929 words), split by rank (#1 got 531 words, #5 got 266); the median source contributed 377 words and selection levelled off at ~540 words, so coverage fell with length (61 % of pages under 1,000 words vs 13 % over 3,000), with a strong lead bias. *Measured, private data, directional.*[^dj-chunks] **Do:** verdict, specs and differentiators in the first ~500 words.
- **Protect the answering sentence.** In one ablation (1 query, 1 page, n=188), one specific factual sentence was worth +4.10 ± 0.10 of 5 rank places, 20 % of the text held 80 % of the gain, the brand name repeated 4× added +0.10 ± 0.10 (≈ 0) and a tangential sentence cost −0.20. *Measured, single case, paid tool.*[^dj-ablation] **Do:** find the sentence that answers, keep it specific, cut off-topic promotional lines; repetition doesn't help.
- How these engines read pages: [AI assistants](../how-it-works/ai-assistants.md), [Google AI features](../how-it-works/google-ai-features.md).

> **Conflict:** the reading unit ranges from a ~200-character card to per-chunk scores and ~540-word grounding caps — [details](../reference/conflicts.md#how-much-of-a-page-do-ai-engines-read)

> **Conflict:** Google says "no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces" — [details](../reference/conflicts.md#writing-for-embeddings-and-chunking)

## 8. Original statistics pages
- LLMs add "statistics" and the current year to research fan-outs. A statistics site with no verified data drew ~72,000 ChatGPT-User requests (of 600k+ bot hits) in 30 days, 10k+ ChatGPT sessions and 330+ referring domains despite a Google demotion. *Practitioner, one-site case; author works for an AI-visibility vendor.*[^mt-stats]
- **Do:** publish genuinely original, sourced data — for example a yearly "[category] statistics" page from your own sales, returns or survey data — with source and year next to each number and a real yearly refresh ([§4](#4-one-topic-in-depth-dated-honestly)). Use it for PR too ([links and digital PR](../channels/links-and-digital-pr.md)).
- **Sourced statistics and quotes in the research.** The GEO paper (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) found that adding citations, quotations and statistics raised visibility in generative answers "by up to 40%", while keyword stuffing had little effect; Liu et al. (2023) found only 51.5 % of generative-search sentences fully supported by their citations. *Academic, relayed; both predate today's production engines.*[^ls-context] **Do:** back claims in guides with named sources, real quotes and numbers — as a reader benefit, not a ranking lever.

## 9. Headlines and summaries for editorial guides
- For editorial headlines, the first 8 words carry most weight; the on-page article headline can run up to 110 characters (this is not the `<title>`); a summary sentence follows the H1; question subheads and consistent links to topic hubs help; Discover favours large images, with people where relevant. *Practitioner, publisher context.*[^gn-article]
- **Do:** for magazine-style guides, name the subject in the headline's first words, follow the H1 with a one-sentence summary, link each guide to its topic hub, and use a large lead image ([titles and snippets](../technical/titles-and-snippets.md), [Google Discover](../channels/google-discover.md)).

## 10. The strip-the-logo test
- Content that could belong to anyone builds the category, not the brand: remove the logo and ask whether a reader could still tell whose page it is. *Essay; relays NN/g and System1, primaries not read.*[^brief-distinct]
- **Do:** before publishing a guide, strip the logo and check it carries something only you could publish — own data, tests, photos, a stated opinion; otherwise add it or don't publish ([homepage and brand](homepage-and-brand.md), [scaling content and templates](../situations/scaling-content-and-templates.md)).

## 11. Lists and "best" pages are a labelled format
- Google labels listicles and top-N pages as their own format and measures them; commerce pages are split into product page, review, comparison, buying guide and top-N roles. Self-promotional rankings were the format that declined in early 2026. *From the leak; the decline is practitioner-reported.*[^rs-intent] Back every list with stated criteria and how you tested, and match the role the results show ([Google ranking](../how-it-works/google-ranking.md#10-intent-decides-the-kind-of-page-per-market)).
